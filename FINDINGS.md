## G1: the spiking world model does not clear the validity gate on `v6`

31 August 2026. Eight seeds per model, dark condition, 1600 steps, batch 32,
sequence length 64 (the parent's budget, its loss, its environment and its
estimator). `runs/gate_snn_v6.json`, `runs/gate_rate_v6.json`.

| model | verdict | beating persistence | median ratio | macro |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `snn`, 128 neurons | PARTIAL | 1/8 | 1.2822 | `gateSnnV6MedianRatio` |
| `rate` twin, 128 neurons | FAIL | 0/8 | 1.4491 | `gateRateV6MedianRatio` |
| conv GRU (parent, same environment) | PASS | 8/8 | 0.6534 | `parentArmv6Ratio` |
| RSSM (parent, same environment) | FAIL | 0/8 | 2.1223 | `parentRssmv6Ratio` |

A ratio above 1.0 means the model predicts blacked-out frames *worse* than
holding the last frame it was shown. Both new models sit above 1.0; the single
`snn` seed that passes does so at 0.9892, which is a 1% margin. Both sit
between the parent's working GRU and its failed RSSM.

Per PREREG G1, PARTIAL is analysable only under `--allow-partial`, and it was
not passed. `spike_torus.py` refused:

    validity gate runs/gate_snn_v6.json reports PARTIAL (1/8 beat persistence,
    median ratio 1.2822). Analysis refused.

**H1, H2, H3 and H4 are therefore not run.** That is the designed behaviour,
and the refusal is the reportable event.

### It is not a budget artefact

The first thing to rule out, since matched budget was the parent's defence of
its own RSSM negative. Percentage fall in training loss over the final quarter
of training, eight seeds each:

| model | median | range | macro |
|---|---|---|---|
| `snn` | 1.5 | -7.1 to 10.9 | `trainSnnV6FinalQuarterPctMedian` |
| `rate` twin | 4.2 | -2.0 to 15.5 | `trainRateV6FinalQuarterPctMedian` |
| conv GRU (parent), same 1600 steps | 10.6 | 3.3 to 23.3 | `parentGruV6FinalQuarterPctMedian` |

The distributions overlap, so this does not show the spiking model had stopped
improving outright. An earlier read of three seeds suggested a cleaner
separation than eight seeds support, which is what the macro rule is for. What
it does show is that the spiking model was improving about seven times more
slowly than the GRU at the same point in the same budget, having started from
the same loss (0.0318, `trainSnnV6MseFirst`) and ended between 0.0097 and
0.0197 (`trainSnnV6MseLastMin`, `trainSnnV6MseLastMax`) against a held-out
blackout MSE of 0.01157 (`trainSnnV6DarkMse`) versus the GRU's 0.00569
(`parentGruV6DarkMse`).

So the budget does not favour the GRU, and extending it is not obviously the
fix. But a longer run is not ruled out by this either, and PREREG requires
saying so rather than claiming convergence the data does not show. A budget
extension would be a declared amendment.

### It is not a dead population

`runs/surrogate_diag_v6.json`, all 16 checkpoints: `diagSnnV6NDegenerate` 0 and
`diagRateV6NDegenerate` 0, against the PREREG item-2 rule. Median firing rate
0.499 (`diagSnnV6FiringRate`), silent-neuron fractions at most 0.13. The
populations are alive and heterogeneous. The exclusion rule did not fire, and
was not allowed to be applied by eye.

### The surrogate diagnostic looks alarming and was not acted on

Post-training median absolute margin is 1.4748 (`diagSnnV6MarginP50`) against a
fast-sigmoid surrogate that has fallen to 1% of peak by 2.25. The emission
gradient is therefore running at a small fraction of peak across the whole
population.

This is the trap this project inherited, arriving mirror-imaged. In the parent
the statistic looked healthy while the model was dead; here it looks dead while
the model demonstrably learned (loss `trainSnnV6MseFirst` to
`trainSnnV6MseLastMin`-`trainSnnV6MseLastMax`, firing rates near 0.5). Neither reading is available from the statistic, which is why PREREG
forbids gating on it. Nothing was tuned in response to it. `surrogate_width`,
the threshold calibration, the learning rate and the budget are all as
pre-registered.

## H3: the control is weaker than intended, and says what it can

The rate twin exists to isolate discreteness. It fails at least as badly as the
spiking model (0/8 against 1/8, median ratio 1.4491 against 1.2822), which
reads as: the failure is not caused by the emission being binary.

**That reading has to be discounted, and by a measured amount.** The twin
isolates discreteness only if its emission is genuinely graded, and a
saturating sigmoid at a large learned margin is not: `sigmoid(4 x 1.9)` is
0.9995, a spike in all but name. Measured fraction of emitted values strictly
between 0.02 and 0.98:

| model | median across seeds | best seed | macro |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rate` twin | 0.2383 | 0.4027 | `diagRateV6GradedMedian` |
| `snn` | 0.0 by construction | 0.0 | `diagSnnV6GradedMedian` |

So roughly three quarters of the twin's emissions are saturated. It is a
partial control, not a clean one, and "both fail, therefore not discreteness"
is supported but **not established**. What is established is narrower and still
useful: the failure is not specific to the *hard* threshold, since a model that
retains up to 40% graded emissions fails too.

A clean version of this control is a twin with a much smaller
`surrogate_width`, or an emission that cannot saturate. That is a new run, and
under PREREG it is an amendment rather than a substitution: this result stands
as recorded whatever the cleaner twin says.

## A1: the graded control failed its own validity condition

31 August 2026, under amendment A1, which was written before the run.
`runs/gate_ratesoft_v6.json`, `runs/surrogate_diag_v6.json`.

**Check the control before reading its verdict.** A1 fixed in advance that
`rate_soft` counts as a graded control only if its median
`frac_emission_graded` reaches 0.70.

| model | graded fraction, median | range | macro |
|---|---|---|---|
| `snn` | 0.0 by construction | 0.0-0.0 | `diagSnnV6GradedMedian` |
| `rate` twin | 0.2383 | 0.0703-0.4027 | `diagRateV6GradedMedian` |
| `rate_soft` (A1) | 0.2741 | 0.2212-0.9308 | `diagSoftV6GradedMedian` |

It reaches 0.2741. **The condition fails**, so per A1 `rate_soft` is reported as
another partially-saturated model and **the spiking-machinery versus
architecture separation is not licensed.** Its gate verdict is recorded below
but carries none of the weight A1 hoped to put on it.

### Why it failed, which is the interesting part

The control was graded at initialisation and *trained itself out of being one*.
Median absolute margin over training, `snn` against the graded control:

| model | median margin, first log | last log | growth | macro |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `snn` | 0.71 | 1.47 | 2.1x | `trainSnnV6MarginGrowth` |
| `rate` twin | 1.59 | 2.78 | 1.7x | `trainRateV6MarginGrowth` |
| `rate_soft` | 1.21 | 49.48 | 40.8x | `trainSoftV6MarginGrowth` |

`sigmoid(0.25 * margin)` leaves the graded band at a margin of about 15.6. The
control was built with a 16-fold narrower emission width precisely to stay
inside that band, and the model drove its margins up 40.8-fold until it was
outside it: 7 of 8 seeds finish above that boundary
(`trainSoftV6NSeedsAboveGradedBand`), against 0 of 8 for `snn` and 0 of 8 for
the first twin. The comparison is across seeds rather than drawn from one,
because a single-seed trajectory is how this line of work last shipped a wrong
number.

**And the one seed that stayed graded is the one that did not work.** Seed 5
holds `frac_emission_graded` at 0.9308 (a genuinely continuous control) with
75% of its neurons never firing. It is the single checkpoint in 24 that the
PREREG item-2 exclusion rule catches (`diagSoftV6NDegenerate` 1). In this
architecture, across these eight seeds, staying graded and being functional did
not co-occur.

### Its gate verdict, recorded but not load-bearing

`rate_soft` FAIL 0/8, median ratio 1.5909 (`gateSoftV6MedianRatio`). Excluding
the degenerate seed it is 0/7. All three v6 models:

| model | verdict | beating | median ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| `snn` | PARTIAL | 1/8 | 1.2822 |
| `rate` | FAIL | 0/8 | 1.4491 |
| `rate_soft` | FAIL | 0/8 | 1.5909 |
| conv GRU (parent) | PASS | 8/8 | 0.6534 |

### What A1 actually bought

Not the separation it was written for. What it bought is the reason that
separation was unavailable: **three models initialised across a 16-fold range
of emission width all converge to an effectively binary code, and all fail the
gate.** The pull toward binarisation is strong enough to defeat a control
designed specifically to resist it.

This is an observation, not a pre-registered test, and it is stated here as one.
It also disposes of the idea that a differently-shaped emission is a cheap fix:
the emission shape at initialisation does not survive training.

estimator; `v6` and `v7` share renderer, image size, colours, sensor noise and
action space, and differ only in that `v6`'s joints wrap while `v7`'s saturate

|---|---|---|---|---|

| `rate` v7 | PARTIAL | 7/8 | 0.5286 | `gateRateV7MedianRatio` |
| `snn` v6 | PARTIAL | 1/8 | 1.2822 | `gateSnnV6MedianRatio` |
| conv GRU v7 (parent) | PASS | 8/8 | 0.3006 | `parentArmv7Ratio` |
| conv GRU v6 (parent) | PASS | 8/8 | 0.6534 | `parentArmv6Ratio` |

`v7` is easier for both architectures, but only the spiking model crosses 1.0:

`snn` seeds land in a narrow band.

## H4: FAILS on the primary view, and the failure is the informative part

31 August 2026. `runs/spike_family_v7_snn.json`, `runs/spike_family_v7_rate.json`.
Each cell analysed under its own gate: `snn` PASS 8/8, `rate` PARTIAL 7/8 under
`--allow-partial`, recorded in its JSON as the declared deviation it is.

**The A3 floor is applied first.** The parent's GRU on this environment returns
translation 8/8 on both joints (`parentFamV7J1NTranslation`) with winning
residuals of 0.1285 and 0.0733 (`parentFamV7J1WinResidual`,
`parentFamV7J2WinResidual`).

### The pre-registered test fails

Primary view `r` (the state the decoder reads, the analogue of the GRU hidden
vector) on the `snn` cell, the only cell with a clean gate:

| joint | verdict | winning residual | margin | clears A3 floor | macro |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | rotation 6/8 | 0.9103 | 0.0331 | no | `famSnnV7RJ1WinResidual` |
| 2 | rotation 7/8 | 0.9341 | 0.0273 | no | `famSnnV7RJ2WinResidual` |

H4 predicted translation on >= 7/8 seeds. It got translation on 2/8 and 1/8, so
**the family is not recovered and H4 fails.** The "rotation" verdict is not a
verdict either: at a residual of 0.9341 the fitted transform is no better
than applying none, and the margin separating the two families is 0.0273. On the state
the decoder reads, the criterion finds nothing.

### The emitted component finds the parent's answer, in both cells

The `s` view (the emitted spike vector, the same 128 dimensions as every other
single-component view) returns **translation unanimously**, on every seed, both
joints, in both cells: 32 of 32 fits.

| cell | view | verdict | winning residual | margin | clears A3 floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `snn` | `s` | translation 8/8, 8/8 | 0.6952 / 0.7165 | 0.2645 / 0.2587 | no, just above |
| `rate` | `s` | translation 8/8, 8/8 | 0.2672 / 0.2319 | 0.2277 / 0.3312 | **yes** |
| `rate` | `i` | rotation 7/8, translation 5/8 | 0.4977 / 0.5221 | 0.1963 / 0.1993 | **yes** |
| conv GRU (parent) | its one state | translation 8/8, 8/8 | 0.1285 / 0.0733 | -- | -- |

Two 128-dimensional components of the same frozen state, the same criterion, the
same eight seeds, both clearing the pre-registered floor, disagree on joint 1:
`s` returns translation 8/8 at a margin of 0.2277, `i` returns rotation 7/8 at
a margin of 0.1963. Pointing the instrument at a different part of the state
changes which group the actuator is said to induce.

### Why the pre-registered statistic cannot be quoted for this

The `agree` counts exist (`famRateV7FamAgreeI` is 15/16 and
`famSnnV7FamAgreeV` is 3/16) and **none of them is readable.** They are
agreement with the reference view `r`, and `r` clears the A3 floor in neither
cell (0.7622 and 0.9103). A3 fixed this before the data arrived: agreement with
a view that fits nothing is agreement with a coin flip, in either direction. A
count of 15/16 against a noise reference is not evidence of invariance, and
3/16 is not evidence of dependence.

So the pairwise comparison above is **post-hoc in form.** It follows the logic
A3 pre-registered but not a statistic A3 specified, and it is offered as a
hypothesis for a confirmatory test, not as a passed one.

### Three things that weaken it, stated plainly

**It is the deviation cell that carries the result.** The `snn` cell has the
clean 8/8 gate and produces no view that clears the floor. The cell that
produces the floor-clearing comparison is the `rate` twin, whose gate was
PARTIAL. The clean cell yields nothing; the compromised cell yields the finding.

**A bounded code may favour translation for representational reasons.** The `s`
view is bounded to [0,1] and non-negative in both models. A translation family
may fit such a code better than a rotation family because of what it can
represent, not because of what the dynamics do. Nothing here separates those,
and the unanimity of `s` across both cells is as consistent with the first as
the second.

**Per H3, none of this is about spiking.** The floor-clearing comparison is in
the rate twin. Whatever the effect is, it is a property of a multi-component
recurrent state, which is what H3 said the honest description would be.

### What would settle it

A cell with a clean gate, a reference view that clears the floor, and the
comparison pre-registered as the primary statistic rather than reconstructed
after the fact. `v6` cannot supply it, because no cell there cleared the gate at all.

## A4: confirmed, on fresh seeds, in a clean-gated cell

31 August 2026. `runs/a4_verdict.json`, scored by `src/confirm_a4.py`, whose
criteria were transcribed from A4 and whose six tests were written against
synthetic reports before any fresh seed finished training. The verdict below is
the string that program emitted.

Eight fresh seeds (8-15) of `snn` and `rate` on `v7`, trained into
`runs/conf_v7/`. The discovery checkpoints were not touched. Only the seeds
differ.

### Both confirmatory cells cleared the gate

| cell | confirmatory (seeds 8-15) | discovery (seeds 0-7) |
|---|---|---|
| `snn` | PASS 8/8, ratio 0.4749 | PASS 8/8, ratio 0.4569 |
| `rate` | **PASS 8/8**, ratio 0.6669 | PARTIAL 7/8, ratio 0.5286 |

The twin passed cleanly this time, so the comparison below is not resting on a
declared deviation as the discovery version was. The `v7` gate result also
replicates independently on fresh seeds.

### Primary prediction: CONFIRMED in the `rate` cell

A4 predicted, before training: on joint 1, with both views clearing the A3
floor, `s` returns translation on >= 6/8 seeds **and** `i` returns rotation on
>= 6/8.

| cell | `s` joint 1 | `i` joint 1 | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rate` | translation 8/8 | rotation 6/8 | **confirmed** |
| `snn` | residual 0.697 | residual 0.7571 | uninformative --- neither clears the floor |

The `snn` cell came back **uninformative**, which A4 pre-registered as an
outcome that is neither a pass nor a failure: its views fit too little to
compare, as in discovery. The effect is readable only in the twin, both
times.

**The `i` side landed right at the 6/8 bar** (7/8 in discovery). The
prediction is met at its pre-registered minimum, not comfortably.

### Both secondary predictions hold, in both cells

**P1**: the `s` view returns translation on >= 7/8 seeds, both joints: holds
in both cells (`a4SnnP1Holds`, `a4RateP1Holds`), at 8/8 everywhere. Combined
with discovery this is 64 of 64 fits across two runs, four cells and sixteen
seeds, matching the parent GRU's verdict on this environment.

**P2**: the primary view `r` does not clear the A3 floor: holds in both cells,
at residuals 0.896 and 0.9514 (`snn`) and 0.7711 and 0.7495 (`rate`). The state
the decoder reads (the analogue of the GRU hidden vector, and the obvious
place to point the criterion) finds nothing, on every cell measured.

### Three further disagreements, flagged as new observations

The `rate` cell produced three floor-clearing disagreements beyond the
pre-registered pair (`a4RateNOtherDisagreements`), all involving the `v` view:
`s` against `v` on both joints, and `i` against `v` on joint 2. The scorer marks
these `is_the_preregistered_pair: false` so they cannot be counted as part of
the confirmation. They widen the phenomenon beyond one view pair and are
untested predictions for a future run.

### What is now established, and what is not

**Established.** On `v7`, in a cell that passed its gate 8/8, with a directional
prediction fixed before the data existed and scored by code: two equally-sized
components of the same frozen model's state return opposite answers about which
one-parameter group an actuator induces. "The dynamics use this coordinate" is
under-specified unless the state component is named. That is the claim this
project was built to test, and it now has a pre-registered confirmation.

**Not established, and the leading alternative.** *Why* the views disagree. `s`
is bounded to [0, 1] and non-negative; `i` is an unbounded integrator. A
translation family may fit a bounded code better because of what that code can
represent, not because of what the dynamics do. If so, the disagreement is a
fact about representational geometry rather than about dynamics. That still
establishes the under-specification, since the criterion's answer still depends
on the analyst's choice, but changes what the finding is *about*.

This confound is untouched by A4 and was named in A4 before the run. The
decisive test is an environment where the correct family is **rotation**: if the
`s` view still returns translation there, its verdict tracks its own geometry
rather than the dynamics, and P1's 64/64 is an artefact. Every wrapping
environment in this project is `v6`, and no `v6` cell has cleared the gate, so
that test is not currently available.

**The cheap partial substitute**, not yet run: the untrained control, already
listed in the PREREG controls. If randomly initialised models also return
translation 8/8 on the `s` view, the verdict is geometric and not learned.
`spike_family` has no `--untrained` path yet; `spike_torus` does.

**Per H3, none of this is about spiking.** The effect is readable in the rate
twin and uninformative in the spiking cell, in both runs. It is a property of a
multi-component recurrent state.

## A5: the untrained control clears the confound, and breaks my floor

31 August 2026. `runs/a5_untrained_verdict.json`. Random weights, no gradient
step, the same pre-gradient threshold calibration the trainer applies, gate
bypassed on the record. Scored by `confirm_a4.score_a5`, which implements A5's
own criterion rather than A4's. Reusing A4's scorer would have answered a
different question.

### The pre-registered outcome: LEARNED, both cells

A5 asked whether the `s` view's translation verdict exists before any learning.

| cell | untrained `s`, joint 1 | trained `s`, joint 1 | A5 verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `snn` | translation 5/8, residual 0.9754 | translation 8/8, residual 0.697 | learned |
| `rate` | translation 7/8, residual 0.9047 | translation 8/8, residual 0.2821 | learned |

At random initialisation the `s` view fits **nothing** (0.9047 against a
no-transform reference of 1.0), and after training it fits at 0.2821. The
majority survives at random init in the `rate` cell (7/8) but without a fit, so
the pre-registered condition for "geometric" is not met in either cell.

**The bounded-code confound is not supported.** Geometry alone does not produce
the trained verdict. This does not prove a dynamical account; it removes the
leading alternative to one.

### The unplanned finding: the A3 floor does not discriminate

In the `rate` cell, **8 of 10 untrained (view, joint) fits clear the A3 floor**
(`a5RateNUntrainedClearingFloor`): `v`, `i`, `r` and `all`, on both joints. One
of them, the untrained `i` view at joint 1, fits at 0.2946, **better than the
same view achieves after training** (0.5898).

A randomly initialised network with no gradient steps clears the bar I
introduced in A3 as a quality check. So clearing the floor means a fitted
transform beats doing nothing. It does **not** mean the criterion found a
learned coordinate.

**This is a framing error in how I reported A4**, and it is corrected rather
than quietly dropped. I wrote that "two views that each clear the A3 floor give
opposite verdicts", with the floor doing rhetorical work it cannot do. The
floor was never evidence of learning and A5 shows it directly. A note to this
effect is appended to A3 in `PREREG.md` so the error is not repeated.

**A4's substance survives; its framing does not.** The confirmed disagreement is
still a confirmed disagreement on fresh seeds under a pre-registered directional
prediction. What is withdrawn is the implication that clearing the floor
certified either verdict as real.

### What the control shows that is stronger than what was predicted

Untrained, `rate` cell, joint 1. Every view that fits at all agrees:

| view | untrained | trained |
|---|---|---|
| `s` | translation 7/8, no fit (0.9047) | translation 8/8, fits (0.2821) |
| `i` | translation 8/8, fits (0.2946) | **rotation** 6/8, fits (0.5898) |
| `r` | translation 8/8, fits (0.5234) | **rotation** 7/8, no fit (0.7711) |

**At random initialisation the views do not disagree.** They all say
translation. The disagreement between `s` and `i` that A4 confirmed is
*produced by training*: learning flips `i` and `r` from translation to rotation
while `s` stays translation and starts to fit.

That is a better argument for view-dependence being a learned property than the
one A4 was designed to make, and it was not predicted. It is recorded here as
an unplanned observation, not as a confirmed result, and it needs its own
pre-registered test before it is quoted as one.

**A hypothesis it suggests, recorded as one.** An untrained recurrent network is
close to linear, and a near-linear response to an action increment is close to a
constant displacement, that is, a translation. If so, translation is the
*default* verdict for a system that has learned nothing, rotation is the
non-trivial one, and a translation verdict is correspondingly weaker evidence
than a rotation verdict. That would make P1's 64-of-64 translation result the
less informative half of this project's findings and the trained flip to
rotation the more informative half. Untested.

## What this does and does not license saying

**Supported.** On `v6`, whose joints wrap, a current-based LIF world model with
a four-component state and a decoder reading the spike trace does not learn to
predict the arm through a blackout well enough for the used-coordinates
criterion to be applied to it, at the parent's budget, loss and estimator.
Neither of its two twins does either, across a 16-fold range of initial
emission width.

**On `v7`, whose joints have stops, the same architecture passes the gate 8/8**
(`gateSnnV7MedianRatio`), so the `v6` failure is a fact about that environment
and not about the architecture. See the G1-v7 section.

**Not supported, and must not be written.** That spiking networks cannot
support the criterion. The `v7` result refutes the general form directly.
Nor that they cannot support it on wrapping joints in general: one neuron
model, one surrogate, one width, one budget, one readout choice. The root cause
of the `v6` failure is unknown, as it is for the parent's RSSM.

**A correction, logged rather than quietly edited.** An earlier revision of this
file, written before `v7` had run, said "two architecture families have now
failed the gate where the conv GRU passes" without naming the environment. That
generalised past `v6` and `v7` refuted it. The claim now names the environment.

**Confirmed, after A4 and surviving A5.** That the criterion's family verdict
depends on which state component it is pointed at (pre-registered, fresh
seeds, clean gate, scored by code) and that the dependence is *learned*: at
random initialisation the views agree, and training makes them disagree. The
bounded-code alternative is excluded by A5.

**Withdrawn: that clearing the A3 floor certifies a verdict as real.** A5 shows
a random network clears it on 8 of 10 fits. The floor tests that a transform
beats doing nothing, nothing more. A4's disagreement stands; the floor language
around it was wrong.

**Still not separable, after A1.** Whether the failure is caused by the spiking
machinery or by the four-component LIF architecture remains open, because the
control built to separate them saturated. Answering it needs a model whose
emission *cannot* saturate (a bounded-range emission, or a margin penalty)
and that is a further amendment, not a rerun.

**A hypothesis, recorded as one.** Both models reset the membrane potential
multiplicatively by the emitted value every step, and both route the entire
recurrent signal through that bounded emission, so what the state can carry
across a blackout has to survive that squeeze at every timestep. This is
untested. It predicts that a soft-reset variant, or a recurrence carrying `v`
or `i` rather than `s`, would clear the gate. Testing it is a design change and
belongs in a declared amendment, not in a retune of this one.
